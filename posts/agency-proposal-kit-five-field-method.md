## The core method: five fields per finding

Every recommendation in your proposal should carry five fields. If a finding cannot fill all five, it is not ready to show a client.

1. **Observed** — what you actually saw, on which page, on which date. URL included.
2. **Interpretation** — what it plausibly means for the client's business.
3. **Proposed work** — the concrete action you would take.
4. **Acceptance check** — how the client verifies completion without trusting you.
5. **Sequence** — where it sits in a 90-day plan and what must happen first.

The separation matters. Most AI-assisted proposals mix observation and claim, so one wrong detail sinks the document. Separated fields let a client check your evidence without checking you.

## Worked example (real site, real date)

Source: autoincomesys.com, inspected 2026-09-17 in a 1280×900 browser viewport.

- **Observed:** The homepage's accessibility listing contained links to a dozen products followed by hundreds of article links. Rendered document height: ~31,981 CSS pixels.
- **Interpretation:** Breadth may make product selection difficult for a first-time visitor. Note what this does *not* say: neither the length nor the link count proves lost sales.
- **Proposed work:** Short set of buyer-specific starting points on the homepage; full library moved to a dedicated browse page; product preview beside each purchase action.
- **Acceptance check:** A visitor can reach a relevant preview and its working purchase destination from the initial navigation. Commercial impact is assessed from actual purchases and refunds — navigation changes are not labeled revenue gains without that data.
- **Sequence:** Validate the offer and purchase path first; improve the pages buyers actually use; expand only after observing real purchase behavior.

That is the format. Client-checkable, honest about limits, sequenced.

## What this method is not

- Not a technical SEO audit: no crawl budget analysis, no log files, no schema validation suite.
- Not keyword-volume research: no proprietary dataset, no search-volume forecasts.
- Not a revenue promise: nothing in this kit predicts rankings, traffic or income.

Say this to clients too. The honesty is part of the product: agencies lose clients by implying certainty they cannot deliver. A proposal that separates evidence from interpretation survives the client's own expert review.

## The 20-contact test (how to sell this service yourself)

If you productize this method as an agency service, test it before building anything:

1. Cap pre-sale spend and preparation time; write one honest sample from public sources, with dates on every observation.
2. List 20 agencies that visibly sell the relevant service. Use a channel you are allowed to use. Introductions beat cold lists; a reply channel must exist before you promise a pilot.
3. Offer a fixed-scope paid pilot — not a free audit. One domain, three competitors, one revision, a stated delivery window.
4. Count only paid orders as validation. Interest, praise and promises are not purchases.
5. Deliver within the window. Record your real hours. Contribution = price minus data/model costs, payment fees, and your hours valued at your rate. If hours win, the fix is scope, not hustle.
6. Do not scale on two sales. A repeat order inside 30 days is the signal to invest; silence after paid delivery is a product signal, not a personal one.
